> [!definition]
> an ellipse is a shape where the sum of the [[distance]] from the two [[focal point|focal points]] to any point along the edge is constant.
> 
> The standard equation of an ellipse centered at $(h,k)$ whose horizontal [[radius]] is $a$ and vertical [[radius]] is $b$ is
> $$\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1$$
> 

Technically, a [[circle]] is an [[ellipse]], but usually when [[ellipse|ellipses]] are mentioned, a shape with unequal [[radius|radii]] is meant (a circle has two equal [[radius|radii]]).

The major [[radius]] is the longer [[radius]] which connects the [[vertices]] of the [[ellipse]]. The [[focal point|foci]] lie on it. 
The minor [[radius]] connects the co-[[vertices]].

The [[focal point|focal]] length ($f$) is the distance between a [[focal point]] and the center.
$$
f^2=p^2-q^2
$$
We can use the [[focal point|focal]] length to find where the [[focal point|foci]] lies by moving that length along the major axis.
