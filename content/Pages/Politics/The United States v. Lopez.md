# facts
Alfonzo Lopez carried a gun and bullets to his school.
He was arrested and sent to jail because a state law prohibited bringing a gun to school.
There was also a federal law ([[gun free school zones act of 1990]]) that he broke, so state charges were dropped in place of federal charges
Lopez was sentenced to six months in prison.

# arguments
### US arguments
The [[US Constitution|constitution]] does not give the federal government the authority to pass gun legislation.
The [[gun free school zones act of 1990]] was passed based off the [[commerce clause]]. 
+ guns in school lead to violence in schools, which reduces the chance that people will go to those states, reducing commerce
+ gun violence also leads to worse schools and less educated citizens, which hurts commerce

### Lopez's argument
gun regulation is specifically [[reserved powers|reserved]] for the states
connection between the [[commerce clause]] and gun violence is weak at best, tyrannical at worst
[[US Congress|congress]] had no business passing the law

# Decision
[[Supreme Court]] sided with Lopez in 5-4 decision

If [[US Congress|congress]] can use the [[commerce clause]] to regulate guns, what can't they regulate?
> Under the theories that the Government presents in support of [this case], it is difficult to perceive any limitation on federal power, even in areas such as criminal law enforcement or education where States historically have been sovereign. Thus, if we were to accept the Government's arguments, we are hard pressed to posit any activity by an individual that Congress is without power to regulate.
