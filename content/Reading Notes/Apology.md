Socrates' enemies ganged up against him and accused him of impiety, the introduction of strange [[God|gods]], and the corruption of the youth. Plato, wrote a dramatized version of his defense.  

Socrates argues that the charges of impiety and the introduction of strange [[God|gods]] are contradictory. Is he an atheist or not? And his mission of philosopher is from [[God]] himself, and thus disobedience would be true impiety.

Socrates argued that he has no motive for corrupting the youth, because why would anyone want to live around evil and corrupted men as opposed to good men. If he is unintentionally doing so, "he should be educated rather than prosecuted."

During the trial, Socrates points out that many of his pupils are present but not assisting in defense. He does not want compassion or mercy, he wants [[justice]] for himself.

Socrates is ultimately condemned. He says he could've saved himself, but the tactics to do so would've been beneath him. 
> The difficulty, my friends, is not to avoid [[death]], but to avoid unrighteousness; for that runs faster than [[death]] .

> Socrates, old and slow, had been overtaken by the slower runner; his sprightly accusers have been overtaken by the faster.

> Is death a dreamless sleep? Such a sleep is more blessed than most nights and days in the life of even the most fortunate mortal. Is death a journey to another world? How splendid, to be able to meet the glorious dead and to converse with Hesiod and Homer! ‘Nay. if this be true, let me die again and again.’ He has so many questions to put to the great men and women of the past: and in the next world no one will be put to death for asking questions. ‘The hour of departure has arrived, and we go our ways – I to die, and you to live. Which is better? God only knows.’
