Socrates fought in the Peloponnesian war, winning a reputation for bravery. Afterwards, he worked as a public official, pursuing [[justice]] at the cost of unpopularity.

Socrates left no written work. We have plenty of biographies of him, but the trouble is sorting out "sober fact from admiring fiction." His biographers agree that he was "'shabby and ugly, pot-bellied and snub-nosed' but the agreement goes little further than that."

Like the [[sophist|sophists]], Socrates debated rich young men, but unlike them, he charged no fees. He did not instruct, but question, drawing the answer out of his pupil like a baby out of a pregnant woman.

According to the shrine of Delphi, there was no man wiser than Socrates. Socrates himself came to believe this because he saw that he was the only one who believed his wisdom was worthless. Everyone else claimed wisdom and lacked it.

Socrates believed that a lack of morality is simply due to a lack of knowledge. If you knew the right thing to do, you would do it, because everyone wants to live a good. [[life]].
