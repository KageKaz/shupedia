When [[current]] runs through a coil in the same plane as a [[magnetic field]], a net torque is generated. However, once the coil becomes perpendicular to the field, the torque becomes zero ([[magnetic force]]  is directly into the pivot point).  When the coil spins around, now the direction of [[current]] is flipped, meaning the coil will experience a torque in the other direction.

To create a motor, you need to maintain a torque in one direction. Thus a [[commutator]] is used. For example, you could use brushes that connect the coil to the circuit. When the coil is parallel to the field, the brushes are connected. When it spins and become perpendicular, the brushes disconnect, and when it spins back to parallel, the brushes reconnect, maintaining current flow in the same direction.
